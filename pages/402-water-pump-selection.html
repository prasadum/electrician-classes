Head & Flow Calculation

The most critical step in pump selection — get this wrong and nothing else matters

What is Total Dynamic Head (TDH)?

TDH is the total equivalent height the pump must push water against. It combines static elevation difference, velocity losses in pipes, and friction in fittings.


TDH = Static Head + Friction Head + Velocity Head

Static Head = Suction Lift + Delivery Height // vertical distance, source to outlet Friction Head = f × (L/D) × (v²/2g) // Darcy-Weisbach formula Practical thumb: Add 10–15% of static head for friction in well-designed systems

🏠 Domestic Selection


  1. Determine borewell/open-well depth and static water level
  2. Add vertical delivery height (OHT or highest outlet)
  3. Add 10–15% for pipe friction losses
  4. Calculate daily water requirement (200 L/person/day)
  5. Choose pump type: Submersible for borewell, Monoblock for openwell/sump
  6. Select 1-phase motor if HP ≤ 2 (single-phase available), else 3-phase
  7. Include pressure switch + dry-run protection relay

Typical Domestic Setup (Kerala/South India):
Borewell 200–300 ft → 1 HP / 1.5 HP 4" Submersible → 1-phase → Pressure switch + DRP → 1" GI riser pipe

🏢 Commercial Selection


  1. Conduct water audit — daily demand, peak demand, storage
  2. Size OHT / underground sump appropriately (1.5× daily demand)
  3. Calculate TDH including friction in distribution network
  4. Use duty+standby pump configuration (2 pumps on auto-changeover)
  5. 3-phase motors preferred (>3 HP) for reliability and efficiency
  6. Install auto-level controller + float valves
  7. Consider VFD (Variable Frequency Drive) for energy saving in large buildings

Commercial Rule of Thumb:
Always size for 120% of calculated demand. Install 2 pumps with AMF panel for auto-changeover every 24 hours.

Process Water Supply

High flow, low-medium head. Horizontal split-case centrifugal. 5–50 HP range. Closed-loop with expansion tank. Pump curves must be verified against system curve.


Centrifugal High flow

Fire Fighting Pump

Jockey + Main + Diesel standby. IS 15105 compliance. Minimum 2.8 bar at hydrant. Electric pump: DOL starter on dedicated circuit. Never throttle — always bypass.


IS 15105 Life Safety

HP Sizing Formula

Motor Power (kW) = (Q × ρ × g × H) / (η_pump × η_motor × 1000) Where: Q = Flow rate (m³/s) // 1 LPS = 0.001 m³/s ρ = Water density = 1000 kg/m³ g = 9.81 m/s² H = TDH in metres η_p = Pump efficiency (~0.60–0.75) η_m = Motor efficiency (~0.88–0.92) Simplified: HP = (Q_LPM × H_m) / (75 × η) Example: Q=100 LPM, H=40m, η=0.65 HP = (100 × 40) / (75 × 0.65 × 60) = 1.37 HP → Select 1.5 HP

Electrical & Panel Requirements

Single-phase, three-phase, starters, cable sizing, protection devices

🔌

Single Phase (1φ — 230V AC)

  • Used for motors up to 2 HP (max 3 HP with capacitor)
  • Domestic supply available everywhere
  • Capacitor-start-run (CSR) motor typical
  • Protection: MCB 2P, ELCB, voltage guard
  • Starting current: 6–7× full load current
  • Star-delta NOT applicable
  • Auto-transformer starter for larger 1φ motors
⚡

Three Phase (3φ — 415V AC)

  • Used for motors 2 HP and above (always preferred >3 HP)
  • Higher efficiency, lower running cost
  • Squirrel cage induction motor (SCIM)
  • Protection: MCCB 3P, overload relay, phase-failure relay
  • Starting methods: DOL, Star-Delta, VFD, Soft Starter
  • Balanced 3-phase supply mandatory — phase imbalance protection essential

Types of Solar Pump Systems

BLDC Solar Submersible

Brushless DC motor driven directly by solar panels via MPPT controller. Most efficient for low-to-medium power (0.5–5 HP). No battery needed. Pumps only when sun shines.


Best choice ≤5 HP

AC Inverter-based Solar Pump

Solar panels → MPPT inverter → Standard 3-phase AC motor. For >5 HP where BLDC not available. Grid-hybrid option available. Higher system cost.


>5 HP applications

Battery-backed Solar Pump

Solar + battery bank → inverter → pump. Allows pumping at night or cloudy days. Significantly higher cost. Only when grid completely unavailable and storage needed.


Only if critical

Solar Panel Sizing

Panel Capacity (Wp) = (Motor kW × Safety Factor) / System Efficiency Where: Safety Factor = 1.25 (losses, temperature) System Efficiency = 0.85 (MPPT × cable) For 1 HP (0.75 kW) BLDC: Panels = (0.75 × 1.25) / 0.85 = 1.1 kW → Use 4 × 300 Wp panels (1200 Wp) Daily output: 1200 Wp × 5.5 hrs = 6.6 kWh = ~10,000 L/day at 65m TDH
