STEP 01 Why UJT? — Setting the Stage

You already know the BJT — it has 2 junctions and works great for amplification. But what if you only need a device that fires automatically at a set voltage — like a pulse generator or SCR trigger? Do you need all that BJT complexity?

💡 Real-world need: In industrial motor drives, SCR firing circuits, and timers — we need precise, repetitive pulses at low cost. A simpler device that snaps ON at a set voltage is ideal.

Enter the Unijunction Transistor (UJT) — ONE junction, three terminals, and a unique trick called Negative Resistance that no BJT does naturally.

Key geometry fact: The emitter P-dot is positioned closer to B1 than B2. This is why RB1 > RB2 and η (eta) > 0.5.

STEP 03 Equivalent Circuit & Intrinsic Standoff Ratio η

The N-bar between B1 and B2 behaves as a resistor. The emitter-bar interface is a diode. Together they form a simple equivalent circuit:

η = RB1 / (RB1 + RB2) = RB1 / RBB
η (eta) ranges 0.51 to 0.82 · Typical: 0.63 · Note: RB1 > RB2 always
VP = η × VBB + 0.7V    (Peak / Firing Voltage)
UJT fires the instant VE reaches VP. The 0.7V is the emitter diode's forward voltage.
🔢 Worked Example: η = 0.65, VBB = 15V → VP = 0.65 × 15 + 0.7 = 9.75 + 0.7 = 10.45V. The UJT will snap ON the moment the emitter reaches exactly 10.45V.

STEP 04 How UJT Operates — 3 Stages

Operation happens in three clear stages. Click each stage to see the animated current flow in both sections of the bar.

IB2 — upper channel (B2→E, always present) IE — hole injection at emitter IB1 — lower surge (IB2+IE combined to B1)
🌊 Dam Analogy: Capacitor voltage slowly builds like water rising behind a dam. The moment it reaches the dam top (VP) — it BURSTS through completely and suddenly. UJT snaps ON hard. The water level drops to VV, dam resets, and the cycle repeats.
⚡ Key — Full channel is active:
Upper section (B2→E, RB2): Steady interbase current IB2 flows. RB2 stays roughly constant.
Lower section (E→B1, RB1): Holes injected from emitter flood this section, collapsing RB1. B1 receives the combined surge IB2 + IE. This is why the animation shows amber (upper) and large fast teal (lower) separately.

In Stage 2, Negative Resistance occurs:

Normal resistor: V↑ → I↑ (Ohm's law)
UJT negative resistance zone: V↓ while I↑ — opposite of Ohm! Hole injection reduces RB1 → lower voltage drop → more current → yet more holes → self-reinforcing SNAP-ON action.

STEP 06 Relaxation Oscillator — Key Application

The most important UJT circuit — a relaxation oscillator using just R, C, and the UJT. It generates repeating pulses automatically.

Waveform — Press Play to watch the oscillator cycle:

f ≈ 1 / (R × C × ln(1 / (1 − η)))
R=10kΩ, C=10µF, η=0.63 → f ≈ 4 Hz — visible blink rate!

STEP 07 UJT vs BJT — Comparison

Since your trainees already know BJT, this table locks in the differences clearly.

FeatureBJT (e.g. BC547)UJT (e.g. 2N2646)
Full nameBipolar Junction TransistorUnijunction Transistor
PN Junctions2 — Emitter-Base & Base-Collector1 — Emitter only
TerminalsE, B, CE, B1, B2
Main useAmplificationSwitching / Oscillation
Control methodBase current IBEmitter voltage VE
Negative resistanceNoYes — key feature!
Circuit complexityHigher (bias network needed)Simpler (R + C only)
Typical applicationAmplifier, logic switchOscillator, SCR/TRIAC trigger
Part number (N-type)BC547, 2N22222N2646, 2N2647
N-type bar UJT (most common) vs P-type bar UJT: The standard UJT has an N-type silicon bar with a P-type emitter dot (e.g. 2N2646). A P-bar UJT also exists — bar is P-type, emitter is N-type, supply polarity reverses. However, P-bar UJTs are extremely rare in practice. For all ITI work, UJT means the N-bar type.
When to choose UJT over BJT? Choose UJT when you need a simple, reliable pulse generator that fires at a precise voltage — e.g., firing an SCR or TRIAC in a motor speed control circuit. Just R + C + UJT = complete oscillator. No complex bias network needed.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

✅ UJT = 1 PN junction, 3 terminals (E, B1, B2)
✅ B1 & B2 are ohmic contacts at opposite ends of N-bar
✅ Emitter P-dot is on the side, closer to B1 (so RB1 > RB2)
✅ η = RB1/RBB — determines firing voltage VP
✅ VP = η × VBB + 0.7V
✅ Both bar sections active on firing — B1 gets IB2 + IE
✅ Negative resistance → snap-ON switching action
✅ Used in oscillators, timers, SCR/TRIAC trigger circuits
