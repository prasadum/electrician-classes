Fig 1.1 — EV Charging Power Flow: AC Grid → DB → EVSE → Vehicle OBC or DC Fast Charger → Battery
🔍 👨‍🏫 CLASS OPENING — Module 1 Introduction
Welcome to Module 1. Before we look at equipment, understand this diagram completely — it is the foundation of everything. Point to the OBC in the vehicle: this is the key difference between AC and DC charging. In AC charging, the car converts AC to DC inside itself. In DC fast charging, we do that conversion in the big cabinet and push DC directly to the battery. That is why DC is so much faster — we bypass the car's small on-board charger.
Fig 1.2 — EV Connector Types used in India (IS 17017). From left: Bharat AC-001, Type 2 (Mennekes), CCS Combo 2 (Bharat DC-001), CHAdeMO
🔍 🔌 HANDS-ON — Pass the CCS2 Connector Around
Hold up a CCS2 connector as you explain: "Look at this — it is actually two connectors in one body. The top half is a standard Type 2 AC connector. The bottom two large pins carry DC up to 400V. Manufacturers chose this design so one inlet on the car handles both slow AC charging at home and fast DC charging at highway stations. This is now the Bharat DC-001 standard — mandatory for all public fast chargers in India."
Fig 1.3 — Control Pilot (CP) Signal States (IS 16046). EVSE and vehicle communicate through PWM signal before any power flows.
⚠️ ⚠️ CRITICAL — Mode 1 is NOT Recognised by IS 16046 for EV Use
This CP signal is the "handshake" before charging begins. Draw attention to State C: only when the vehicle pulls the CP to +6V does the EVSE switch on the power relay. If anyone asks why you cannot just plug in directly — this is why Mode 1 (no CP) is not recognised under IS 16046 for EV charging, and is effectively restricted by CEA Regulations 2010 (Reg. 42) which require proper earthing and protection for all socket outlets. There is no CP handshake, no earth verification, no RCD protection in the cable — the car cannot communicate readiness.

KEY CONCEPT — AC vs DC Charging

AC Charging: EVSE supplies AC power → Vehicle's On-Board Charger (OBC) converts AC to DC → charges battery. Power limited by OBC capacity (3.3–22 kW).

DC Charging: Charger unit converts AC→DC internally → supplies DC directly to battery, bypassing OBC. Very fast (22–350 kW). Requires 3-phase supply.

AC ചാർജിംഗ്: OBC ഉപയോഗിച്ച് AC-ൽ നിന്ന് DC-ലേക്ക്. DC ചാർജിംഗ്: ചാർജർ യൂണിറ്റ് DC ബാറ്ററിയിലേക്ക് നേരിട്ട് നൽകുന്നു.

⚠️ Mode 1 — Not Recognised by IS 16046; Restricted under CEA 2010

IS 16046 does not cover Mode 1 — it defines only Modes 2, 3, and 4. CEA Regulations 2010 (Reg. 42) require earthing and protection for socket outlets used with appliances, making bare socket EV charging non-compliant. 🔍 🏠 DEMONSTRATION — Wall Box Physical Inspection
This is what your customer will have on their garage wall. Point out every component: the LED status strip at the top — green means ready, blue means charging, red means fault. The Type 2 socket with shutter — always spring-loaded, water-resistant to IP44 minimum. The four mounting screws in the corners — these must go into wall plugs or timber noggins, not plasterboard alone. A 7 kg charger pulling free from a wall is a very serious hazard.
Fig 2.2 — Protection Devices and Accessories. MCB, RCCB (30mA Type A), SPD, EV cable, ICCB (emergency portable), conduit. All IS-rated.
⚠️ ⚠️ RCCB TYPE SELECTION — Type A Mandatory for AC EVSE
Hold up the RCCB and explain Type A vs Type B: Standard household RCDs (Type AC) cannot detect pulsating DC fault currents that EV chargers can produce. IS 16046 requires Type A minimum — and for DC fast chargers, Type B. If you fit a plain Type AC device, it may not trip on an EV fault current. This is a life-safety issue. Always check the label — Type A has a half-sine wave symbol.
💡 💡 INSTALLATION TIP — Kerala Weather Considerations
Pro tip from the field: Always install the EVSE cable entry from the bottom — never the top. If water ingress occurs (Kerala monsoon!), it drains out rather than pooling on the PCB. Use IP55 enclosures outdoors, IP44 indoors minimum. Seal all cable glands with self-amalgamating tape, not just push-fit.
⚠️ INSTRUCTOR CLARIFICATION — Socket vs EVSE vs ICCB
A household 15A wall socket is NOT an EV charger. Three scenarios students must clearly distinguish:
  1. Wall socket alone (Mode 1): No CP signal, no RCD, no earth check. IS 16046 does not define this mode for EV charging. CEA 2010 Reg. 42 restricts this. Do not use.
  2. Wall socket + ICCB cable (Mode 2): The ICCB box in the cable adds CP pilot, RCD (30mA), and earth continuity check. The socket itself is just power supply — the ICCB does the protection. IS 16046 Pt.1 covers this. Permitted for emergency/travel use only; not recommended as primary charger.
  3. Dedicated Wall Box EVSE (Mode 3): Fixed dedicated circuit, MCB, RCCB Type A, CP/PP pilot in the EVSE unit, earth monitoring. IS 16046 Pt.1 & Pt.2. Recommended for all permanent home installations.
📌 Key exam point: Mode 2 requires ICCB in the cable — the socket socket alone contributes nothing to safety. Mode 3 has all protection in the EVSE unit. Mode 4 (DC fast) has protection in the off-board charger.

Home Charger — Step-by-Step Installation Procedure

ഗൃഹ ചാർജർ ഇൻസ്റ്റലേഷൻ — ഘട്ടം ഘട്ടമായ നടപടിക്രമം
घरेलू चार्जर इंस्टॉलेशन — चरण-दर-चरण प्रक्रिया
1

Site Assessment & Load Survey

Check existing DB capacity. Measure available load (sanctioned load minus existing connected load). Verify earthing continuity (<1Ω). Confirm single-phase 230V supply.

ലോഡ് അനുവദനീയ ശേഷി — DB ക്ഷമത — ഭൂ-ബന്ധ തുടർച്ച പരിശോധിക്കുക
2

🏛️ 🏛️ CALCULATION — 3-Phase Supply Sizing for 50 kW Charger
Three-phase power for this charger requires a dedicated circuit sized for the full rated current. Step-by-step calculation (show this to KSEB):
1. Full load current: I = P ÷ (√3 × VL × PF) = 50,000 ÷ (1.732 × 415 × 0.95) = 73.3 A per line
2. A single dedicated EV charger = demand factor 1.0 (runs at full load). Design current = 73.3 A
3. MCB/MCCB: Select 80A or 100A TP MCCB, Type C/D curve (63A is under-rated for 73A load — do NOT use 63A)
4. RCCB: 100A Type B RCCB (Type B mandatory — DC leakage current possible in fast charger)
5. Cable (Cu XLPE, 40°C ambient, IS 732 Table 4): 25mm² = 87A rated → select 25mm² Cu 4-core XLPE armoured

🔍 🔍 SITE INSPECTION — Step-by-Step Protocol
Before any tool touches a wall, you must:
1. Check the KSEB meter loading — ask to see the last 3 months bills. If average consumption is near the sanctioned load, you need a load enhancement application.
2. Measure earth resistance with an earth tester — not with a multimeter! You need a 3-point or 4-point earth tester (Megger, Fluke 1625). Reading must be ≤ 1 Ω as per IS 3043 for EV installations.
3. Inspect the DB panel — is there a spare way? What is the current MCCB/MCB rating? Is the neutral bar insulated?
Document everything with photos. Your photos protect you legally.
🏛️ 🏛️ KSEB PROCESS — EV-3 Tariff Sanction (Kerala Specific)

Voltage Drop Calculation — IS 732

वोल्टेज ड्रॉप गणना — IS 732

Voltage Drop Formula — Single Phase

VD = (2 × L × R × I) / 1000

Where:
L = Cable length (metres)
R = Resistance (Ω/km) from cable table
I = Load current (A)

Max allowed VD = 5% of supply voltage
= 5% × 230 = 11.5V max

Example: 7.4 kW, 32A, 25m run, 6mm² Cu
R = 3.33 Ω/km (for 6mm² Cu)
VD = (2 × 25 × 3.33 × 32) / 1000
VD = 5.33V (2.3%) ✓ OK

If VD >5%: Increase cable cross-section or reduce run length. Never compromise on cable size to save cost.

Common Installation Mistakes — DO NOT DO

ഇൻസ്റ്റലേഷൻ തെറ്റുകൾ — ഒരിക്കലും ചെയ്യരുത്
सामान्य स्थापना गलतियाँ — कभी न करें

❌ Undersized Cable

Using 2.5 mm² for a 7.4 kW charger. Will overheat, degrade insulation, cause fire. Always size correctly per IS 732 + voltage drop check.

കേബിൾ ചെറുതാക്കരുത് — അഗ്നിബാധ സാധ്യത

❌ No Dedicated Circuit

Sharing EV circuit with lights, AC or other loads. Causes nuisance tripping, incorrect protection, overloading existing wiring.

ഒറ്റ സർക്യൂട്ടിൽ EV + മറ്റ് ഉപകരണങ്ങൾ — ഒരിക്കലും വേണ്ടാ

❌ Wrong RCCB Type

Using Type AC RCCB (household) for EV circuit. May not trip on DC pulsating fault. Must use Type A minimum (Type B for DC EVSE).

Type AC RCCB — EV-ന് ഉപയോഗിക്കരുത്

❌ Poor Earthing

No earth electrode or earth resistance >10Ω. EV charger body becomes live on fault. Fatal shock risk. IS 3043 mandates <1Ω.

ഭൂ-ബന്ധം ഇല്ലാതെ — ഇൻസ്ടോൾ ചെയ്യരുത്

❌ Reversed Polarity

Phase connected to Neutral terminal. Causes charger malfunction, shock hazard even when switch is OFF. Always verify with polarity tester.

L-N മാറ്റി ഇട്ടാൽ — അപകടം — ടെസ്റ്ററുകൊണ്ട് പരിശോധിക്കുക

❌ Outdoor Cable Unprotected

PVC cables run outdoors without conduit or armour. UV, mechanical damage, and moisture ingress degrade insulation rapidly in Kerala humidity.

ഔട്ട്ഡോർ — കൊണ്ടൂയിറ്റ്/ആർമർ ഇല്ലാതെ PVC കേബിൾ — കേരളം ഈർപ്പം

Fault Diagnosis — Common EV Charger Faults

ചാർജർ തകരാർ കണ്ടെത്തൽ — പൊതു ഫോൾട്ടുകൾ
चार्जर दोष निदान — सामान्य खराबियाँ
Fault / Error Symptom Likely Cause Test / Check Remedy
RCCB Tripping
RCCB ട്രിപ്പ്		 Charger loses power on plug-in Earth leakage from OBC, cable fault, moisture in connector Megger test cable, check connector IP seal, test RCD with tester Dry connector, replace cable, check OBC. If persistent: Type B RCCB
CP Signal Error
CP ഫോൾട്ട്		 Charger shows "No Vehicle" despite EV connected CP wire broken, loose contact at connector, corroded pin Measure CP voltage at EVSE connector: should transition A→B (9V) on plug-in Clean connector pins, replace cable, check EVSE CP circuit
Over Voltage Trip
OV ഫോൾട്ട്		 Charger stops, OV error code KSEB supply voltage >260V (low-load night) Measure supply voltage at meter. Log over 24 hrs. Contact KSEB. Install OV/UV protection relay. Voltage stabilizer.
Under Voltage
UV ഫോൾട്ട്		 Slow charging, charger error KSEB supply <200V, loose terminal, long cable Measure voltage at charger terminals (not just meter) Check cable connections. Contact KSEB for voltage improvement.
Earth Fault
ഭൂ-ബന്ധ ഫോൾട്ട്		 Charger body tingling / shock, RCCB trips PE conductor broken, earth electrode corroded, No earth Measure earth resistance (Megger earth tester) Restore PE connection, replace electrode, add more electrodes
OCPP Offline
OCPP ഓഫ്‌ലൈൻ		 Charger shows "Unavailable", won't start No internet (SIM/WiFi issue), CSMS server down, wrong config Ping CSMS server. Check SIM data. Check Charger log files. Enable offline local auth list. Fix network. Reconfigure OCPP URL.
Temperature Fault
ഊഷ്മാവ് ഫോൾട്ട്		 Charging stops midway, OTP alarm High ambient temp, blocked vents, fan failure, summer Check enclosure vents. Measure internal temp via display. Shade charger from direct sun. Clean filters. Replace fan if faulty.
MCB Tripping
MCB ട്രിപ്പ്		 Complete power loss to charger Cable overload (undersized), loose busbar, short circuit Check cable sizing. Measure cable temperature after running. Upgrade cable to correct size. Tighten all terminals. Replace MCB if weak.
