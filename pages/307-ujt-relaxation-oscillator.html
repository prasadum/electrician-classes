What is a UJT?

A Unijunction Transistor (UJT) is a three-terminal semiconductor device with one emitter (E) and two bases (B1, B2). It has a unique negative resistance characteristic that makes it ideal for relaxation oscillators.

The intrinsic standoff ratio η (eta) is the key parameter: η = RB1 / (RB1 + RB2), typically 0.5 to 0.8 for common UJTs like 2N2646.

How the Oscillator Works

Phase 1 – Charging: Capacitor C charges exponentially through R1 toward VBB. When VC reaches VP (= η·VBB + VD ≈ 8.26V), the UJT emitter-B1 junction switches into low resistance (NDR region fires).

Phase 2 – Discharge: C discharges rapidly through the low-resistance E–B1 path and R4, creating a current spike. Voltage at R4 (B1 output) goes positive, voltage at R2 (B2 output) spikes positive.

Phase 3 – Reset: When VC falls to VV (~2V), the UJT reverts to high resistance. C starts charging again. The cycle repeats continuously.

Key Formulas

VP = η · VBB + VD
η = RB1 / (RB1 + RB2)
f ≈ 1 / (R1·C·ln(1/(1−η)))
T = R1 · C · ln(1/(1−η))
RBB = RB1 + RB2 (4–10 kΩ)
VD ≈ 0.7 V (junction drop)
For our circuit: η=0.63, VBB=12V → VP=8.26V, f≈11.2 kHz

Applications

UJT relaxation oscillators are used as: SCR/Thyristor trigger circuits, timing circuits, sawtooth wave generators, pulse generators for industrial control, voltage-to-frequency converters, and sweep circuits in older oscilloscopes.

💡 The output spikes at B1 and B2 are ideal for triggering SCRs (Silicon Controlled Rectifiers) in power control applications — a very common use in motor speed controllers and lamp dimmers.
