What is an Induction Cooker?

An induction cooker heats cookware directly through electromagnetic induction. The glass-ceramic surface stays cool — only the ferromagnetic pot gets hot via eddy currents.

Core Principle

High-frequency AC through a flat coil generates a rapidly alternating magnetic field → induces eddy currents in the ferromagnetic pan base → eddy currents heat the pan by Joule heating (I²R).

Key Requirements

  • Cookware must be ferromagnetic (cast iron, magnetic stainless steel).
  • Flat bottom, diameter ≥ 12 cm for pan detection.
  • Mains supply: 230 V AC, 50 Hz.

Construction & Components

1. Glass-Ceramic Plate

  • Schott Ceran® — low thermal expansion, withstands 700°C.
  • Transparent to magnetic field; cool to touch during cooking.

2. Induction Coil

  • Flat spiral of Litz wire — reduces skin effect at high frequency.
  • Diameter 140–180 mm; Inductance 50–200 µH.

3. Ferrite Core

  • Concentrates magnetic flux upward into pan; prevents coil overheating.

4. IGBT

  • Main power switch; switches coil current at 20–40 kHz. Rated 600 V, 20–40 A.

5. Resonant Capacitor

  • Forms LC resonant circuit with coil; enables soft-switching (ZVS).
  • Typical: 0.27–0.47 µF, 1200 V AC polypropylene.

6. Control PCB + MCU

  • Generates PWM for IGBT; pan detection; NTC temperature monitoring; display & input.

Why Litz Wire?

At 20–40 kHz, the skin effect forces current to the conductor surface. Litz wire (many fine insulated strands) maximises surface area, drastically reducing AC resistance and coil heating.

Why Ferromagnetic Pan Only?

Ferromagnetic materials (µr >> 1) intensify magnetic flux and increase eddy current density. Non-ferrous pans (aluminium, copper) have µr ≈ 1 — negligible eddy currents → no useful heating.

Operating the Induction Cooker

Before First Use

  • Place on a flat, stable, heat-resistant surface with ≥10 cm clearance on all sides.
  • Verify supply voltage matches nameplate (230 V ± 10%).
  • Test pan with a magnet — it must stick to the base firmly.
