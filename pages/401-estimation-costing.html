What is an Estimate?

An estimate is the method of calculating the cost of material and labour required for a wiring installation. It helps arrange funds, explore market availability of materials, and plan the job systematically before execution.

💡
5% Contingency: Always add 5% of the total cost as contingency for unexpected expenses during construction.

🔢 11 Steps of Estimation

Systematic approach to prepare a wiring estimate from plan to final cost.

1
Position Electrical Points
Mark the position of all electrical points (lights, fans, sockets) in each area of the house based on consumer requirements. Prepare on the building plan.
2
Prepare Wiring Layout
Prepare a detailed wiring layout on the building plan showing conduit runs, DB positions, switch board locations, and all circuit routes.
3
Calculate Total Connected Load
Add up all wattages of connected equipment. Decide whether single-phase or 3-phase supply is needed. Current = Total Watts ÷ 240V.
4
Prepare Circuit Diagram
Draw cubical wiring model showing connections, number of lighting and power circuits. Helps verify that each branch circuit does not exceed 800W (5A branch circuit).
5
List of Electrical Accessories
Prepare complete list of switches, sockets, ceiling roses, MCBs, DBs, fans, light fittings, conduit boxes and all electrical accessories to procure.
6
Calculate Cable Lengths
Calculate length of different sizes of cable required based on load, voltage drop allowed, and number of cables in each circuit run. Include vertical runs (roof run) and horizontal runs.
7
Calculate Conduit/Batten Lengths
Calculate length of batten / PVC casing & capping / metal conduit / PVC conduit required based on load, voltage drop and number of cables in each run.
8
Hardware List
Prepare list of hardware items – screws, nails, GI wire (binding), saddle clamps, conduit clips, junction boxes, PVC bushes – needed to execute the job.
9
Labour Charges
Calculate labour charges for the entire wiring job. Example: Electrician = ₹150/day × 2 days = ₹300; Helper = ₹90/day × 2 days = ₹180. Total labour = ₹480.
10
Cost of Accessories & Materials
Calculate cost of all accessories, base materials (PVC conduit, batten, cables, hardware) at schedule of rates. Sum = Total material cost.
11
Total Cost with 5% Contingency
Total Cost = Material Cost + Labour Cost + 5% Contingency. This final figure is submitted as the estimate for approval and fund arrangement.

🏠 Understanding "Runs" in Estimation
H
Horizontal Run
Cable/conduit running along the wall horizontally, usually at 2.5m height or on ceiling slab. Measured from switch board to the farthest point in each room. Example: Run J = (2.5+0.9+0.8) × 2 = 8.4m
R
Roof Run (Ceiling Run)
Cable/conduit running on or inside the ceiling/roof slab – the vertical portion from wall to the ceiling fixture (fan, light). For concealed wiring, roof runs must be planned before the roof slab is cast. Example: Horizontal run J = 150cm = 1.5m
V
Vertical Drop (Loop)
Cable dropping vertically from ceiling conduit to switch board, socket, or distribution board. Measured as height of wall drop, typically 0.8–0.9m per end.
🧱 Wiring Systems

Select based on location, consumer preference, and KSEB/DISCOM regulations.

1. Batten Wiring
Domestic (Old)
VIR/TRS cables clipped on wooden battens along walls and ceiling. Simple installation, easy maintenance. Least protection – not suitable for damp or industrial areas. Being replaced by conduit wiring.
2. PVC Casing & Capping
Office / Computer
PVC channels (casing) with snap-on covers (capping) carrying cables along walls. Good appearance, easy access. Preferred for offices, computer labs, and commercial areas where surface finish matters.
3. PVC Conduit Wiring
Domestic / Commercial
Cables enclosed in PVC conduit pipes – either surface-mounted or concealed in walls/roof. Most popular in modern construction. Concealed wiring must be planned before roof slab is cast. Good protection, neat appearance.
4. Metal (GI) Conduit Wiring
Industrial / Hazardous
Cables in galvanized iron conduit. Maximum mechanical protection. Used in factories, workshops, oil refineries, chemical plants, and hazardous locations. Conduit itself acts as earth. More expensive than PVC conduit.

⚠️
Important: Concealed PVC conduit wiring must be started and completed BEFORE the roof slab is cast. The electrician should coordinate with the civil engineer well in advance.
🏘️ Domestic / Commercial / Industrial

Estimation requirements and regulations differ by installation type.

Domestic Residential Wiring Estimation

For a typical residential house. Load is single-phase (230V, 50Hz). Each branch circuit is limited to 800W (5A). Separate circuits for lighting, fans, and power sockets.

✔
Branch circuit load ≤ 800W (5A). Lights + fans on lighting circuit; sockets on power circuit.
✔
Cable: 1mm² copper for lighting/fan circuits; 1.5mm² copper for socket circuits.
✔
Main switch, energy meter, and DB at point of entry. IC cutout on service main.
✔
All conductors at height ≥ 2.5m along walls or ceiling.
✔
Third pin of all wall sockets must be earthed; earth conductor min GI 14 SWG or 1.4mm² Al.
✔
Add 5% of total cost as contingency in the final estimate.
Sample Domestic Load Calculation

Assuming: 2 Tube lights (50W each) + 3 Light points (60W each) + 3 Fans (60W each) + 3 Sockets (100W each)
Total = (2×50) + (3×60) + (3×60) + (3×100) = 100+180+180+300 = 760W
760W < 800W ✓ → Single branch circuit OK
Current = 760 / 240 = 3.17A → Use 1mm² Cu cable

Commercial Commercial/Office Wiring Estimation

For shops, offices, malls, and commercial establishments. Higher load density, more circuits, 3-phase distribution, sub-main DB required. PVC casing & capping or conduit wiring commonly used.

✔
Load up to 100kVA: 415V, 3-phase, 4-wire LT supply from DISCOM.
✔
Load 100kVA to 1000kVA: 11kV HT supply. Customer installs own transformer.
✔
Separate and distinct circuits for lighting, power, air conditioning, computer loads.
✔
DB located at load center for minimum voltage drop; sub-DBs for each floor/section.
✔
Power factor correction capacitors may be needed for air conditioning loads.
✔
PVC casing & capping preferred for computer rooms; metal conduit for server rooms.
💼
Commercial Tip: In commercial estimation, include cost of ELCB/RCCB for each circuit, surge protection devices, and cable trays for false ceiling areas. Always price per point for large projects.
Industrial Industrial Wiring Estimation

For factories, workshops, manufacturing plants. 3-phase, 415V supply. Heavy power circuits, motor starters, distribution centres. GI conduit wiring mandatory for all power circuits. Load balancing across phases critical.

✔
Power circuits for loads of 3000W (3kW) and above. All power equipment: Iron-clad switches + GI conduit.
✔
Induction motors ≥ 3HP must be 3-phase type.
✔
Motors up to 5HP: DOL (Direct-on-Line) starter. Motors above 5HP: Star-Delta or Auto-transformer starter.
✔
Load balanced across all 3 phases at main DB and branch DBs.
✔
Flexible conduit ≤ 1.25m. Minimum cable cross-section: 1.25mm² Cu or 1.5mm² Al for power wiring.
✔
Main switchboard: 1m clear space in front; passage way ≥ 2m height if rear space > 75cm.
✔
Fuse rated for starting current (normally 2× full-load current of motor).
✔
Load > 1000kVA or motors >150HP: HT supply at 3.3kV, 6.6kV or 11kV. Load > 3000kVA: EHT supply.

🧮 Worked Calculation Example

Complete domestic wiring estimate – 2 room house, 2 circuits

📌 Problem: 2-Room House Estimation
C1
Circuit 1 – Room No.1
1 Tube light (1200mm, 50W) + 2 Light points (60W each) + 2 Fan points (60W each) + 1 Socket (6A, 100W) = Total 390W
C2
Circuit 2 – Room No.2
1 Tube light (50W) + 1 Light point (60W) + 1 Fan point (60W) + 2 Sockets (100W each) = Total 370W
Total Connected Load

Circuit 1: 390W + Circuit 2: 370W = 760W
760W < 800W → Both rooms can share ONE branch circuit ✓
Current = 760 ÷ 240 = 3.17A
→ Cable selected: 1mm² copper (or 1.5mm² aluminium)

Cable Length Calculation

Horizontal run J = 150cm = 1.5m (rough value for each end) → Total: 25m for runs 2–5
Conduit run J = (2.5+0.9+0.8) × 2 = 8.4m
Conduit run A = (1.5+0.9+0.8) × 7 = 22.4m
Run B = 1.1 × 2 = 2.2m
Run C = (1.05×4 + 1.85×2) = 7.9m
Run D = (1.5+0.8+0.9) × 3 = 9.6m
Run F = (1.8+0.9+0.8) × 5 = 17.5m
Run G = 2.2 × 2 = 4.4m
Run H = (2.2+0.8) × 2 = 6.0m
Run K = (0.9+0.2) × 2 = 2.2m
Total cable required = 80.6m → say 90m of 1mm² copper cable

Earth Wire

Copper 14 SWG for earthing runs K, H, F, E, D, A = 16.9m → say 20m or 600g
